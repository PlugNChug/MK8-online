## Switch Pro Controller motion with Steam Input (#164)

On Windows, the Steam-mapped SDL gamepad identified the Bluetooth Switch Pro Controller and delivered buttons and sticks, but exposed neither motion sensor. A desktop SDL process could read the physical accelerometer and gyroscope at the same time. The integration starts that reader through the [Windows desktop shell](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/shell/samples-execinexplorer) when Steam presents one eligible gamepad. It sends complete sensor pairs to the assigned player and clears them when input stops. The reader rediscovers the controller after disconnect and exits with the game.

Windows regressions cover native sensor precedence, player assignment and clearing old pairs after reconnect. In a separate lifecycle test, Explorer launched the reader, the reader published its heartbeat and it exited after its owner. The reader also cleared stale samples and accepted new pairs when a virtual Pro Controller disconnected and reconnected. The earlier manual reader was verified in a race with one Bluetooth Pro Controller and Steam Input enabled. `gameplay_verified=false`.

The first hardware reconnect restored sensor samples, but GameInput exposed the physical Pro Controller and two Valve records. GameInput proxies occupied the first slots, and the Steam mapping received motion as player 2 or 3. A short wait restored player 1 once, then repeated hardware reconnects accumulated more copies and pushed the Steam mapping to player 4. The runtime now uses Steam's mapped device and the desktop reader's physical device count to distinguish one Pro Controller from multiple controllers. It keeps the duplicate GameInput entries out of player slots while Steam recreates the device and recognizes the same controller model when SDL reports a new attachment. The native regression exercises late SDL discovery and repeat reconnects with duplicate records.

In the next hardware test, the log showed that samples returned to player 1, but the player reported that tilt still recovered inconsistently. The reader now retries sensor activation when an attached controller stops supplying complete accelerometer and gyro pairs. It clears the old pair, starts a new sensor generation and limits retries during a continuous stall. The game logs separately when the Steam mapping or the fresh sensor pairs go away. The native SDL regression fails with the earlier reader and passes with the retry. [SDL Switch driver](https://github.com/libsdl-org/SDL/blob/release-3.4.18/src/joystick/hidapi/SDL_hidapi_switch.c)

The retry build recovered buttons and tilt consistently when the player waited for Steam's disconnect notification. Reconnecting before the notification could still leave motion unavailable. Restarting the SDL joystick subsystem only when an attached device stayed silent recovered two of three rapid hardware reconnects. The reader now restarts discovery after a prolonged gap in fresh samples, including when SDL fails to supply a replacement attachment. The native regression exercises both an attached device that stays silent and a replacement that never appears.

On November 27, 2026, the player confirmed two rapid Bluetooth reconnects in Time Trial with Steam Input enabled. Buttons and tilt returned after a delay on the first attempt and immediately on the second. The Windows MK8 4.0.1 runtime (`d23b68bb`) logged the first sensor connection reopening and motion returning to player 1 both times.

## Adjustable stick dead zone

The launcher and F1 Controls menu share `stick_deadzone` in `mk8d.ini`. The setting takes a whole percentage from 0 to 50 and defaults to 0 for existing profiles. The HID state writer filters each controller stick as a pair of axes. Outside the dead zone it rescales the remaining range along the same direction, preserving full travel. It applies after Joy-Con axis rotation and before keyboard, touch or scripted motion is added.

On Windows, the compiled SDL test fed virtual gamepads through the prepared HID path. At 20%, the primary stick's `(3900, −4200)` became neutral. A paired Joy-Con still reported the diagonal `(1708, −1708)` from `(6000, −6000)`. Both seats kept full travel, and a button and scripted axis still registered. The native and overlay settings tests exercised the new row, and Edge verified launcher persistence. [Issue #167](https://github.com/Vnnc1/MK8-online/issues/167)

In MK8 4.0.1 on Windows, the 0.6.3 baseline runtime (`61dca153…`) and the host-only rebuild (`f9afeb67…`, set to 20%) both reached the frame-3300 checkpoint in Mario Kart Stadium Time Trials and exited normally. The two runs used the same guest library and scripted inputs with isolated saves. Physical stick feel and Linux runtime behavior remain unverified.

## Controller discovery — continuation of #50

A failure was found in the initial enumeration: only `ids[0]` was tried for player 1; if SDL could not open it, the others were forwarded to `open_extra`, leaving player 1 without a controller. `controller_discovery.py` applies a common routine to the enumeration and to GAMEPAD_ADDED, trying each valid device until player 1 is filled. Already open IDs are ignored, even if player 1 disconnects while player 2 remains, without stealing that controller on a duplicate event. Keeps `pads_from_p2`; an open failure reports the real SDL error, without calling name APIs with a null handle. A successful open updates the buttons and signals a style change.

## Local split screen: the 2-player selection froze — 2026-11-14

**Reproduced symptom.** Main menu → Multiplayer → Grand Prix → 50cc → controller applet (`[hid] controller support (players 2..2): 2 player(s)`) → character selection for 2 players. The image stayed **still**: seven 30 s captures, identical byte for byte. The game kept drawing the same frame (~80 draws per frame) and its threads ran at 60 Hz with none busy (maximum 7% CPU). The two players' buttons reached the runtime (`MK8_TRACE_PADS`), but not even player 1's cursor moved, and player 2's did not even appear. The game's controller manager (`f_70005fc03c`) read style and state of IDs 0 and 1 normally, and the mode without Mii was not the cause: with the real Mii resources the screen also froze.

**Cause.** On the Switch, `nn::hid::ShowControllerSupport` opens a system applet: the game leaves the foreground and, when the applet closes, the applet manager enqueues `FocusStateChanged`. The runtime answered the applet immediately and enqueued nothing, and MK8 left the scene paused waiting for focus to return.

**Fix (`toolchain/controller_applet.py`, default; `MK8_NO_APPLET_FOCUS=1` turns it off).** When answering the applet, `am.c` gains `mk8_am_push_message` and enqueues `FocusStateChanged`, like the end of the real applet. `GetCurrentFocusState` remains "in focus".

**Proof, on the same build:**
- **Off:** `race-grand-prix-10948eao`, four identical captures.
- **On:** `race-grand-prix-v2nka2gx`. Player 1 moved the yellow cursor to Luigi, player 2 appeared with the cyan cursor, chose and confirmed, and both went to the kart selection, each on their own panel.
- **Split-screen race:** `race-grand-prix-esulv10d`, Mario Kart Stadium with two Luigis, each half with its own camera, items, coins and position (2nd/3rd at frame 9600, 1st/4th at frame 12000). Exit 0, no `FATAL`.
- **Performance:** 58.71 FPS in the split race (frames 8000–11990), p95 19.5 ms, p99 20.9 ms, one 232 ms translation spike. That is ~2× the draws per frame of solo mode.

**Test:** `tests/test_controller_applet.py` runs the real message queue of the prepared `am.c`: after the boot message, the applet enqueues `FocusStateChanged`, signals the event, respects the limit of 16 and restarts on reboot. It also checks that the hook sits in `ShowControllerSupport`, after the response, and respects `MK8_NO_APPLET_FOCUS`. Runtime `--runtime-only`; 40 related tests passed.

**Limits:**
- **Controllers:** player 1 is the keyboard + the first controller, and each extra controller is the next player. With **keyboard + 1 controller** there is no player 2: both command player 1, and the applet answers 1 player when the game asks for 2. Playing as two requires two physical controllers. A "keyboard is player 1" option is left as a next step.
- **Not validated:** 3–4 players, Battle and split VS, physical controllers (the test used player 2 scripted through `LARECOMP_INPUT2`), full split GP and audio. `gameplay_verified` remains false.
